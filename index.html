The clinical gap

Angiography shows the lumen. Clinicians need to see the wall.

Angiography is a 2D outline of the lumen and cannot characterise plaque. Intravascular imaging helps, but today's tools were built for large coronary arteries and leave fine wall features unresolved.

100–150 µm

IVUS resolution

About ten times coarser than OCT. Thin fibrous caps and micro-calcifications are easy to miss.

15–30 µm

1300-nm OCT axial resolution

Current clinical OCT systems run at about 1300 nm, which limits how clearly fine wall structure and subtle thrombus can be seen.

NURD

Torque-coil friction

Friction between the coil and sheath causes non-uniform rotational distortion in tortuous vessels, which is worst in the brain.

US$2,000+

Per single-use catheter

Disposable cost slows adoption, especially in high-volume and regional centres.

The platform

Three parts, designed together.

One console serves both coronary and neurovascular catheters. Software turns raw data into depth-uniform, measurement-ready images at the point of care.

01 · CONSOLE

Portable 1060-nm console

A swept-source imaging engine at 1060 nm gives two to three times finer axial resolution than 1300-nm systems.

  • Frame rateup to 400 fps
  • Scan range22 mm

Ultra-slim imaging catheter

Liquid-shaped micro-optics in a catheter of 0.5 mm OD or less, sized for tortuous intracranial vessels. A coronary-optimised variant is also planned.

  • Working length1800 mm

DeepField HR & analysis suite

Physics-informed machine intelligence sharpens images across the full imaging depth in near real time, and an analysis suite handles measurement and reporting.

Core technology

Three inventions behind the platform.

Each one addresses a known limit of current catheters: how the optics are made, the trade-off between depth and resolution, and rotational distortion.

01 · Liquid-shaping optics

Freeform micro-lenses made by surface tension, not polishing.

Curable optical droplets settle into a defined shape on wettability-patterned substrates. This gives monolithic freeform lenses with designed aberration control and no angle-polishing or critical alignment. A side-lens version forms the lens directly on the fibre for ultra-slim side-view probes.

<1 nmRMS surface roughness
≈1 hourLens forming, assembly and QC
0 stepsPolishing or critical alignment
0.4 mmSmallest OD demonstrated
02 · DeepField HR

High resolution across the full depth, not just at the focus.

A hybrid diffractive and freeform-reflective micro-lens forms a needle-like, multi-focal beam. A physics-informed neural network then applies digital dispersion compensation, digital adaptive optics, ISAM reconstruction and self-supervised denoising. Together they keep lateral resolution close to the diffraction limit across the whole lumen.

≤10 µmFull-field lateral resolution (target)
10–100×Depth of field vs. GRIN or ball lens
03 · Torque-coil-free drive

Removing the main cause of rotational distortion.

The metal torque coil, and the friction it causes, is replaced with low-friction functional coatings and fluid-assisted torque transmission. Lower internal drag gives smoother, faster rotation and a more flexible probe that can reach small, tortuous vessels.

~400 rpsStable rotation (design goal)
No coilSimpler, lower-cost assembly

Clinical applications

One platform for coronary and neurovascular use.

We start where high resolution changes procedural decisions: high-volume cardiovascular centres and comprehensive stroke centres.

Cardiovascular

Coronary PCI guidance

Characterise plaque before stenting, then check expansion, apposition and edge dissection afterwards, at a resolution that shows thin caps and micro-calcifications.

  • Plaque characterisation
  • Stent sizing
  • Apposition
  • Edge dissection
Neurovascular

Intracranial vessel-wall imaging

A catheter of 0.5 mm or less that can reach narrow, tortuous intracranial arteries, to image intracranial atherosclerotic disease, thrombus and device apposition during neuro-interventions.

  • ICAD
  • Thrombus
  • Intracranial stenting
  • Device apposition
Roadmap

From prototype to first-in-human.

Development is planned verification-first, with quality-system groundwork and biocompatibility testing ahead of clinical studies.

  1. 2024

    Company founded

    Luminvue Medical Limited incorporated in Hong Kong as a CUHK spin-off.

  2. 2025

    Prototype & animal imaging

    Portable 1060-nm console and liquid-shaped catheters built. Ex vivo and in vivo porcine imaging completed.

  3. 2026

    Characterisation & quality

    Bench characterisation, ISO 13485 / IEC 60601 gap assessment and ISO 10993 biocompatibility testing.

  4. 2027

    Partners & design v2

    International exhibition debut, clinical site engagement and next-generation console and catheter.

  5. 2028+

    Clinical studies

    Early feasibility studies at partner centres, followed by registration in China and other markets.
